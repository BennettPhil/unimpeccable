# Radical Italian

Archizoom Associati (1966–1974). Superstudio (1966–1978). Gruppo Strum, UFO, 9999. Florence and Milan, 1960s–70s. design as **critique**, not product.

these groups didn't want their work mass-produced. they wanted it to argue. they drew impossible cities, infinite grids, monumental cubes covering the earth. their renderings were collage — photos of mountains and people and ruins inside an architectural fiction.

Memphis came out of this lineage. but Radical was colder. more conceptual. more black and white. more text-heavy.

## VISUAL MARKERS

- monumental scale: a single object dominates the composition
- infinite grids extending past the frame (Superstudio's "Continuous Monument")
- black and white as default, with sparing color
- photo collage: real photographs of people, landscape, ruins composited into invented spaces
- text overlay: manifesto fragments printed across the image
- isometric / axonometric drawings, never perspectival
- the GRID itself as a motif — repeated, oppressive, neutral

## PALETTE

minimal. Radical Italian was usually black + white + one accent (often red or yellow).

- charcoal `#1A1A1A`
- newsprint `#E8E2D0`
- single accent: riso red `#FF4438` OR riso yellow `#FFE800` OR cobalt `#1B3FB2`

NEVER three or more colors at once. NEVER an acid magenta moment. this is not Memphis.

## TYPE

- workhorse: Times New Roman or Georgia
- shouter: Arial Black or Impact
- ALL CAPS section headers
- long-form manifesto-style body copy
- footnotes in smaller workhorse text
- text frequently OVERLAID on images at high contrast

## STRUCTURE

- infinite grid as the load-bearing layer: a 12-column grid with visible 1px black gridlines extending past content
- isometric blocks projected at 30° — color blocks drawn as if 3D solids
- centered axes more acceptable here than in other schools (Radical liked symmetry as oppression, not as comfort)
- BIG empty zones balanced with VERY dense text blocks
- captions in tiny workhorse, set against the grid

## MOTIFS

- infinite grid (1px solid black, 32px or 48px cells, extending past visible content via overflow)
- isometric cube floating in space, drawn in pure 1px line work
- photo-collage rectangle: photograph cropped harshly, placed at unexpected angle
- text-block-as-monument: a paragraph of body copy set inside a black box with paper-white text
- numbered manifesto items (1. 2. 3.) styled as serif numerals at large size

## RULES SPECIFIC TO THIS SCHOOL

- no acid colors. one accent maximum.
- type is dense, not decorative. read this school for long-read pages, not landing pages.
- imagery is photographic AND drawn — not pure illustration, not pure photo. composite.
- the page should feel like a manifesto pamphlet from 1969, not a poster.
- Carson moment if used: a single overlaid headline across a photograph. that's it.

## NAMED REFERENCES

- **Superstudio — "The Continuous Monument" (1969)** — a single rectilinear grid structure spanning the earth
- **Superstudio — "Twelve Cautionary Tales for Christmas" (1971)** — utopian/dystopian cities, presented as drawings + text
- **Archizoom — "No-Stop City" (1969)** — an infinite homogeneous urban grid as critique of consumerism
- **Ettore Sottsass (pre-Memphis) — Valentine typewriter (1969)** — bright red plastic, mass-produced, ironic
- **Gruppo Strum — "Pratone" lounge chair (1971)** — oversized plastic grass blades; soft surrealism

## WHEN TO REACH FOR THIS SCHOOL

- pages with HEAVY long-form text content (essays, manifestos, position papers)
- about pages with political or philosophical framing
- "why we exist" pages
- press / publication archives
- archives of older work — the school suits anything that frames itself as documentation

## WHEN NOT TO

- landing pages selling a saas (this school refuses commerce)
- product feature lists
- anything where you want playful or pop energy (use Memphis instead)
- single-page interactive demos (this school is contemplative, not interactive)

## SELF-CHECK

- is the page two colors plus one accent, max?
- is there a visible grid extending past content?
- is at least one composition photo-collage with text overlay?
- is the type dense, serif workhorse for body, Arial Black for monumental headlines?
- does the page feel like a 1969 manifesto, not a 2024 landing page?
